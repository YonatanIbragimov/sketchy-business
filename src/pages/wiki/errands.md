Every level has one or two **errands**: a gate with a padlock on it, and somebody standing beside it who needs a
favour before it opens. Walk up and they'll tell you what they need. Do it, and they'll tell you a bit more of the
story, and the gate swings open. There are 77 of them across the eleven chapters.

> "An errand. Someone wants something fetched. Welcome to adventuring: ninety percent errands, ten percent falling."
> *The Narrator*

There's always a checkpoint at the door of an errand room, and what you're after shows at the bottom of the screen
("Three fuses 1/3").

## The five kinds

<div class="table-scroll">

| Errand | What you do | A nudge |
|---|---|---|
| **Fetch** | Find one thing and carry it back to whoever asked. It bobs over your head while you carry it. | Some things are **heavy**: no double jump and no dash while you carry them, so look for the way back that doesn't need either. If you fall, it goes back where you found it. |
| **Gather** | Collect every piece scattered around the room. | Count the lamps over the gate. The last piece is usually the high one. |
| **Rescue** | Someone's locked in a scribbled cage. Hit every padlock (swing the pencil or stomp on it). | Padlocks can be up on ledges; the cage bursts when the last one goes. |
| **Levers** | Throw every lever in the room. | Just walk into them. The gate has a lamp for each lever. |
| **Ambush** | The gate stays shut until every minion is gone, and they come in waves. | Don't relax after the first wave: there's another one. Keep moving. |

</div>

## The rooms

Errand rooms come in a few shapes, so they're never just a flat corridor:

- **Tiers**: ledges stacked up the room. The thing you want is near the top.
- **Tower**: a spring up to a loft, and a high perch above that.
- **Cellar**: the floor drops into a pit with spikes at the bottom, ledges on the way down, and a spring to bounce back out.
- **Arena**: open ground for the ambushes.

## Tips

- The gate won't let you jump over it, so don't try. Several of us have tried.
- Carrying something heavy? Go round, not over. The way you came in usually works on the way back.
- If you die mid-errand you start again from the checkpoint at the room's door. If you were carrying something, it
  goes back where you found it.

## Who asks

Twenty-five characters in all, from a farmer in the meadows to a frayed page edge at the end of the book. They're all on the
[characters page](/wiki/characters#furrow), under **Errand folk**.

## The story in the errands

The errands carry the story *between* the boss fights: each chapter's mystery comes out a favour at a time. Spoilers,
chapter by chapter:

<details class="spoiler">
<summary>Chapters 1 to 3</summary>

- **1. A Questionable Start.** The farmers of Margin Meadows notice their fields being rubbed clean overnight. Pip's
  grandpa says the Artist hasn't drawn anything new in ages. Miller Grist explains that the Last Pencil is the only
  pencil that can draw something new, and that the Great Sharpener's screws were loosened by someone planning to come
  back for it.
- **2. The Rub-Out.** Sketchy Steve, a half-finished draft, has been locked up because "drafts are mistakes". Dot says
  nobody's finished yet. Wisp sees Smudge coming with a giant eraser.
- **3. Spiral Bound.** Grandpa Coil watched Smudge cross the spine with the pencil. His old map of the far page has the
  castle on it, marked *formerly the Artist's eraser*.

</details>

<details class="spoiler">
<summary>Chapters 4 to 6</summary>

- **4. Clean Slate Keep.** Wobble, the keep's last pencil-drawn servant, has to erase himself "neater" every morning (he
  used to have a nose). The torn portrait shows Smudge brand new, on the Artist's desk: he was the Artist's own eraser,
  until he was replaced.
- **5. Sticky Note Sky.** Memo's letters carry Inkwell Village's cry for help, and Smudge's notes in purple ink, pinned
  to her board, spell out his plan: the Sharpening Works, Blotto, and "Tidy, tidy, TIDY".
- **6. Pencil Pushers.** Nib and Mrs Blotter keep the village going in the dark, and the Mayor's hint about "the Works"
  falls into place.

</details>

<details class="spoiler">
<summary>Chapters 7 to 11</summary>

- **7. Pencil Case Canyon.** Stub the crayon miner: the pencil case is Smudge's barracks. Fuzz says the Artist stopped
  colouring things in. Stub sends a spare sharpener blade on ahead to Foreman Gearsley.
- **8. Grind.** Sprocket and Old Valve ask the question nobody else does: once the pencil's sharp, who gets it?
- **9. Coffee Stain Marsh.** Off the page, Lump and Dunk explain the Artist's side: the Artist sits over the book every
  evening and doesn't draw, because the real pencil went missing *into* the book. The last thing the Artist drew was
  Noggin.
- **10. The Crayon Kiln.** Stump, a burnt-out match, Mrs Glaze, Drip, Kindling and Snuff: the Kiln is where the
  Artist's broken crayons are melted down, and nobody's poured a new one in ages. Wick was the Artist's desk candle,
  blown out the night the pencil vanished and relit by "somebody pink". The Artist's unfinished sketches show a new
  pencil, with notes all round it: core, casing, point...
- **11. The Final Draft.** Quibble and Fray: under white-out nothing is ever really gone, and a crumb of Smudge shows
  he's been erasing himself as he goes, an eraser afraid of being thrown away. Before the last fight, Noggin collects
  the first pages of his own story.

</details>
