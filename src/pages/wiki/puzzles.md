Every level has a room or two that needs thinking rather than jumping. The door ahead opens once the room is
solved. There are 69 of them across the game. Here's every kind, with a nudge rather than an answer.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Puzzle | What it is | A nudge |
|---|---|---|
| Crate steps | A ledge too high to reach, and a crate. | "A hole you can't climb out of, and a crate. Have a think." |
| Two plates | Two pressure plates and only one of you. | "One of you and a crate." |
| Either-or doors | "Opening one door closes the other." | "Think it through backwards." |
| Powered lift | A lift that won't go anywhere yet. | Find what it's waiting for. |
| Lever blocks | "Blue blocks, red blocks. Only one colour is real at a time." Blue blocks have circles, red ones triangles; solid ones are filled in, the others just outlined. | Hit a lever to swap them. |
| Lure the eraser | A pencil wall only an eraser can rub out. | The eraser follows whoever is nearest. |
| Balance scales | Two pans on a beam: the heavier side sinks. | Doodles and crates both weigh something. |
| Buttons in order | Marks over each button. | "The picture's the order. Probably." |
| Combination lock | Dials you hit to turn, 0 to 9. | The code is written somewhere earlier in the level. |
| Connect the dots | Dots that fade if you're slow. | Touch them in order, quickly. |
| Bell memory | Bells ring a tune. Each bell has its own shape (triangle, square, circle, diamond). | Ring it back: land on a bell or swing at it (just walking across doesn't count). A bell lights up while you stand on it. It gets longer. |
| Shell game | Three cups, one key, lots of shuffling. | The right cup has a bent rim. |
| Tic-tac-toe | Nine buttons, against the Smudgelings. | You don't have to win. A draw is enough. |
| Riddle gates | Three doors and a riddle. Stand at one and press up. | Read the riddle twice. |
| Spot the difference | Two panels of doodles. | Stomp the button under the one that's different. |
| Don't press | A big red button. | Sometimes you should. Sometimes you should wait. Listen to the Narrator. |
| Wait door | A door on a timer. | Stand still. Really. |
| Backtrack | A door that never opens. | Have a look at the floor. |
| Draw a bridge | Dotted outlines. | Hit them with the pencil to ink them in. Hit again to rub one out. |
| Page fold | "A dog-eared corner. Fold it." | Each flap folds in a different set of platforms. |
| Mirror page | "That's you, the other way round." | You on one plate, your reflection on the other. |
| The chase choice | Two buttons while the eraser closes in. | The wrong one crumbles. Read the labels. |

</div>

In co-op there are also four [two-player puzzles](/wiki/co-op#two-player-puzzles).

## Reading a room

Every switch is drawn in its world's own material, but the **shape tells you what to do with it**, in every chapter:

<div class="table-scroll">

| Shape | What it wants |
|---|---|
| A diamond paddle with a star | **Hit it** with the pencil (or a headbutt). |
| An oval plate with boot prints | **Stand on it** (or put a crate on it). |
| A box with a handle | **Carry it.** |
| An hourglass | **Wait.** It shows how long is left. |
| A lever on a gear | **Hit it to flip it.** The handle points up-left or down-right. |
| A trapezoid with a down arrow | **Stomp it.** |

</div>

Clues are drawn bold on quiet paper so they read from a distance: **tally marks** over buttons give their order,
a bold **blue line** joins the dots as you link them, and spot-the-difference panels use four simple pictures (a sun, a house, a
tree, a cloud) with exactly one of them changed.
