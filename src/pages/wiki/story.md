In the margin of a sketchbook, a bored Artist drew a doodle. They spent ages on the head and about three seconds on the
body. His name is **Noggin**. He says nothing, he is quietly brave, and his googly eyes do all the acting.

The sketchbook has one treasure: the **Last Pencil**, the only thing in the book that can draw something new. And it has
one menace: **Lord Smudge**, a pompous pink eraser with a monocle, a tiny gold crown and a magnificent moustache. He
leads the Eraser Aristocracy, and he believes that *a blank page is a perfect page*.

So he stole the Last Pencil, so that nothing will ever be drawn again.

Following along, and not helping, is **the Narrator**: sarcastic, theatrical and unreliable. He mocks Noggin's every
move. Whether he's secretly rooting for him is a matter for chapter 10.

## Along the way

- **Dot** is a full stop who ran away from the end of a sentence, because she didn't want anything to end. She never
  finishes her sentences, and she's the only one who can hear the Narrator, which he finds deeply unprofessional
- **Penny** (player 2) is a ballpoint-pen girl from across the gutter, the fold down the middle of the book. She's
  bossy, sure of herself, and made of ink, which Smudge can't erase.
- **Shavings** are scattered through every level. The Narrator says they'll matter. He's the Narrator; he would know.

## The big themes

Drafts, practice and mistakes are part of the drawing. A crumpled-up early draft, the first doodle ever drawn, and a
correction fluid who covers mistakes instead of fixing them all have something to say about it.

Between the big moments, the story comes out a favour at a time: every level has [errands](/wiki/errands), and the
twenty characters behind them each know a piece of what's going on (who Smudge used to be, why the Artist stopped
drawing). The errands page has it chapter by chapter, behind spoiler folds.

<summary>Chapter by chapter (whole story)</summary>

1. **A Questionable Start.** Noggin reaches the Great Sharpener. Smudge ambushes him, steals the Last Pencil and erases the sharpener. Big Pink, Smudge's bouncer, is rubbing out Margin Village, Dot's house included.
2. **The Rub-Out.** In the field of thrown-away drafts, Scrunch (a crumpled early draft of Noggin: "the head came out too small") learns the Artist wasn't throwing him away, just practising. When Smudge comes back to rub out the whole field, Scrunch tells Noggin to run. Noggin escapes across the ink, which erasers can't cross.
3. **Spiral Bound.** Smudge flees to his castle across the notebook's wire spiral, to the far half of the book.
4. **Clean Slate Keep.** In the throne room, Smudge slips on a pencil shaving and falls off the balcony. Noggin gets the Last Pencil back. Sir Reginald Rule reveals Smudge's past: he was the Artist's own eraser, replaced by a shiny new one and dropped in the margin. He's wanted the book perfect ever since, so nobody could replace him again. And the pencil is *blunt*.
5. **Sticky Note Sky.** The balcony ends in thin air. The only way down is across sticky notes stuck to the sky.
6. **Pencil Pushers.** Smudge knocked over the Great Inkwell, and the spill, Blotto, is drinking the village's ink. Smudge, fresh out of the wastepaper basket, blurts out that only the Sharpening Works can fix the pencil.
7. **Pencil Case Canyon.** The road to the Works runs through a giant pencil case full of minions.
8. **Grind.** Noggin sharpens the pencil in the Great Grinder, and Smudge snatches it the moment it's done. Then comes the shredder.
9. **Coffee Stain Marsh.** The shredder throws Noggin clean off the page and onto the Artist's desk.
10. **The Final Draft.** Smudge is erasing the margins on his way to the edge of the page. Old Margin, the first doodle ever drawn, tells Noggin that artists never leave a sketchbook, they just turn the page, and that the pencil is for drawing what happens next, not a better body. At the edge of the page Smudge slips on one of *our* shavings and falls into the wastepaper basket, and the pencil comes home.

**The ending.** With the Last Pencil, Noggin can draw anything. He draws himself a smaller head, then uses the eraser on
the end to undo it. Dot takes her place at the very end of the story ("I'll only be there until the next story
starts"). And the Narrator admits he was rooting for him the whole time.
